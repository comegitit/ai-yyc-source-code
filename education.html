Education: Integrated Artificial Intelligence

An 8 month post-diploma certificate from the School for Advanced Digital Technology at the Southern Alberta Institute of Technology. The program is built for people who already have post-secondary study or working experience behind them, and it treats AI as an organizational problem before a technical one.

Its stated aim is to bridge the gap between technical expertise and business objectives: designing systems around fairness, transparency and accountability, identifying where an AI opportunity genuinely exists, assessing the risk that comes with it, and leading the work with an understanding of the people the system affects. That framing is why I decided to enroll.

What the program covered:
  • Identifying real AI opportunities, assessing the risks attached to them, and making decisions from evidence.
  • Communicating with stakeholders and non-technical audiences, and translating technical work into business terms.
  • A people-first approach to data and to the systems built on top of it.
  • A capstone project delivered against a real industry challenge, with an external partner.
  • Ethical AI system design, with fairness, transparency and accountability treated as design constraints rather than afterthoughts.

The Capstone

SIGNAL: AI-Assisted Credit Monitoring

Project Manager

A proof-of-concept decision-support system for commercial credit monitoring, built in consultation with a major Alberta-based financial institution. SIGNAL turns document intake into rated, evidence-backed early warnings, with every credit decision left to a qualified human.

I led delivery across a four-person team and helped set the boundaries the system works within: no credit approvals, no risk ratings, no live banking data.

The Coursework

Four courses that shaped how I approach an AI initiative. Each page carries the course outline, the assignments I submitted, and the reports behind them.

ARTI 409: AI Management & Maintenance

The operational lifecycle of enterprise AI after launch: monitoring, model drift, audit readiness, and keeping a deployed system aligned with the business objectives it was funded to serve.

See the coursework: ARTI 409, AI Management and Maintenance

ETHI 401: AI Governance & Ethics

Responsible AI principles, how policy and regulation differ across major jurisdictions, and the case studies where governance either held or gave way under commercial pressure.

See the coursework: ETHI 401, AI Governance and Ethics

ARTI 406: Human-Centred AI

Designing and evaluating AI around the needs, values and wellbeing of the people who have to use it, and testing whether it actually serves them rather than assuming it does.

See the coursework: ARTI 406, Human-Centred AI

DATA 440/480: Predictive Analytics

How predictive models are built, validated and applied to business decisions, what their output can responsibly be used for, and where human oversight stays essential.

See the coursework: DATA 440 and 480, Predictive Analytics
